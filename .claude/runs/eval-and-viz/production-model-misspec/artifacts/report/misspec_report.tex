\title{\vspace{-1.2em}Detecting model misspecification in field-level cosmic-shear inference:\\
cross-encoder posterior disagreement versus summary-space out-of-distribution scores\vspace{-0.4em}}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\vspace{-2.2em}
\begin{abstract}
\noindent Neural posterior estimation freezes the forward model into the network, so an observation
containing physics absent from the training simulations yields a confidently wrong posterior with no
internal warning. We compare two label-free misspecification detectors on a production
KiDS-Legacy-like field-level model trained on Gower Street $N$-body mocks: the disagreement (a
Kullback--Leibler statistic) between the posteriors of five independently seeded encoders, and an
out-of-distribution (OOD) score of the observation's learned 8-dimensional summary against the training
summary cloud. Six paired test suites probe the intrinsic-alignment (IA) model, survey depth and the
source galaxy bias $\bg$. On the IA suites the single-encoder kNN OOD score wins (AUROC $\NNlaAurocKnn$
vs.\ $\NNlaAurocKl$ for the five-encoder KL; power $\NNlaPowerKnn$ vs.\ $\NNlaPowerKl$ at 5\% false
alarms) at a fifth of the training cost, and needs only a few hundred reference summaries. An IA
amplitude offset of $\dAia\simeq0.8$--$1.0$ from the training median is detected in half of all mocks,
$\simeq1.1$--$1.3$ in 80\%; a change of IA model form at matched amplitude is invisible. Depth and $\bg$
variations are not detected. The $\bg$ suites are at most mildly outside the training prior and their
calibration is at the in-distribution floor, so the implicit marginalisation over $\bg$ has not failed;
yet the posterior mean tracks the true $\bg$, shifting by $0.8\,\sigma_{\rm post}$ in $\Om$ and $\sig$
in opposite directions for $\bg=1.0$ and $1.3$ ($\Delta\Om=\NDthetaOmegaM$ between them, seven times the
DES~Y3 bound). This bias, of a $\bg$-marginalised posterior at fixed $\bg$ and of a coherent across-bin $\bg$
configuration, is invisible by construction to every detector and sets the real limit of what these
diagnostics protect against.
\end{abstract}

Simulation-based inference with neural compression \cite{jeffrey2021,papamakarios2016} is now the
standard route to field-level cosmology from weak-lensing maps \cite{jeffrey2025}. Its price is that
every simulator assumption is baked into the estimator: if the sky contains physics the training suite
did not, the posterior stays narrow and its calibration, established on held-out simulations, no
longer applies. A detector that flags such an event on the real observation, without knowing the true
parameters, is therefore a prerequisite for a credible result. Two ideas are natural. Independently
trained networks agree where the data constrain them and diverge where they extrapolate, so the
disagreement of an ensemble of posteriors is a misspecification statistic \cite{lakshminarayanan2017}.
Alternatively, the compressed summary of a misspecified observation should fall outside the cloud of
training summaries, a classical OOD problem \cite{sun2022,morningstar2021}. This report puts the two
head-to-head on the same suites with the same statistic, and quantifies the smallest misspecification
either can see.

\section{Model, test suites and detectors}
\paragraph{Inference model.} A hybrid bandpower-plus-map encoder compresses a six-bin tomographic
KiDS-Legacy-like shear observation into an 8-dimensional summary $t$; a normalising flow estimates
$p(\theta\,|\,t)$ over the seven Gower Street cosmological parameters and the two NLA-M
intrinsic-alignment parameters $(A_{\rm IA},\beta_{\rm IA})$ of \cite{wright2025}. Encoder and flow are
pre-trained on a large log-normal suite and fine-tuned on 300 $N$-body cosmologies (multifidelity NPE
with the VMIM compression loss). Five copies were trained from independent seeds and splits; each is a
complete posterior estimator. The training simulations draw the per-bin source galaxy biases
$\bg^{(i)}$ from a Gaussian prior (means $\NBgPriorMeanBinOne$--$\NBgPriorMeanBinSix$, widths
$\NBgPriorSigmaBinOne$--$\NBgPriorSigmaBinSix$ from bin~1 to~6, truncated at $\pm3\sigma$), but
$\bg$ is \emph{not} an inference parameter: the posterior is implicitly marginalised over the $\bg$ prior.

\paragraph{Test suites.} All suites re-simulate the same locked set of $\NNNullCos$ held-out
cosmologies with $\sim$8 footprint-rotation and shape-noise augmentations each ($\NNNullRows$ mocks), so
every comparison is paired at the cosmology level. \emph{In-distribution}: the training physics.
\emph{NLA} and \emph{NLA-$z$}: the mass-dependent NLA-M model replaced by the plain non-linear
alignment model, without and with a redshift power law, at amplitudes $\Aia\in[-6,6]$.
\emph{Variable depth}: position-dependent survey depth. \emph{$\bg=1.0$} and \emph{$\bg=1.3$}: the
galaxy bias pinned to one value in all six bins ($\NGbOnePZeroNCos$ cosmologies $\times$ 2 mocks).
Amplitudes are compared in the KiDS-Legacy $\Aia$ convention: the NLA suites store it directly, and
the NLA-M training amplitude is converted per mock,
$\Aia=\langle A_{\rm IA} f_{\rm red}^{(i)}(\bar M_h^{(i)}/10^{13.5})^{\beta_{\rm IA}}\rangle_i$, giving a
training support $\Aia\in[\NAiaBandLo,\NAiaBandHi]$ (5--95\%), median $\NAiaBandMedian$.

\paragraph{Detector 1: cross-encoder KL.} The five posteriors of one observation are
approximated by diagonal Gaussians in the scaled parameter space and scored by their mean pairwise
symmetric KL divergence. It needs no reference data and no true parameters, but $K\ge2$ trained
encoders. In-distribution its mean is $\NBgpNlaMKlMean$~nats (median $\NKlNullMedian$, 95th percentile
$\NKlNullPNineFive$).

\paragraph{Detector 2: summary-space OOD.} The summary $t$ is whitened with the training-cloud
covariance and scored by its mean distance to the $k=10$ nearest training summaries (kNN,
\cite{sun2022}; $\SNTrainRows$ summaries from $\SNTrainCos$ cosmologies). A \emph{conditional} variant
first removes the parameter dependence, $r=t-\mathbb{E}[t\,|\,\theta]$, and scores the residual; it
needs $\theta$ (the truth in simulations, posterior draws on real data). Scores become right-tail
empirical $p$-values against the held-out in-distribution null. Summaries of different encoders are not
commensurable, so the only cross-encoder combination is of $p$-values; the recommended combiner is the
mean of the five calibrated $p$-values, recalibrated against the null (``mean-$p$''). Fisher, Stouffer,
GLS-weighted Stouffer \cite{linsullivan2009} and density-of-states \cite{morningstar2021} combiners
were also tested and none beats it by more than one bootstrap standard deviation. The ceiling is
structural: the five encoders' scores of the same mock are correlated at $\rho=\NRhoCrossEncoderNull$
(the scatter is the mock's own shape noise and cosmic variance, seen by every encoder), so any
five-view combination gains at most $\sqrt{5/(1+4\rho)}=\NSnrGainFive$ in signal-to-noise.

\paragraph{Common footing.} Every detector is scored by the area under the ROC curve (AUROC,
\cite{bradley1997}) of variate against in-distribution mocks, and by its detection power at a 5\%
false-alarm rate (the fraction of variate mocks above the null's 95th percentile). Rows are correlated
augmentations of far fewer cosmologies, so all intervals are a paired cluster bootstrap over
cosmologies \cite{efron1993}: 1000 draws resample the $\NNNullCos$ cosmologies and take all their
in-distribution and variate rows (row-level intervals would be $\sim$3$\times$ too narrow). As a
truth-requiring, suite-level reference we also quote the TARP \cite{lemos2023} calibration error
$\mathcal{C}$.

\subsection{KL versus OOD, head-to-head}
Figure~\ref{fig:h2h}a and Table~\ref{tab:main} give the answer. On the two IA suites every detector
separates the variate from the training physics, and the ranking is unambiguous: single-encoder kNN
($\NNlaAurocKnn$~[$\NNlaAurocKnnLo$, $\NNlaAurocKnnHi$] on NLA, $\NNlaZAurocKnn$ on NLA-$z$) beats the
five-encoder KL ($\NNlaAurocKl$~[$\NNlaAurocKlLo$, $\NNlaAurocKlHi$]; $\NNlaZAurocKl$), which beats the
five-encoder mean-$p$ ($\NNlaAurocMeanp$; $\NNlaZAurocMeanp$) and the single-encoder conditional kNN
($\NNlaAurocCondKnn$; $\NNlaZAurocCondKnn$); the intervals of the first three do not overlap. At a
fixed 5\% false-alarm rate the gap widens: kNN detects $\NNlaPowerKnn$ of NLA mocks, KL $\NNlaPowerKl$,
mean-$p$ $\NNlaPowerMeanp$, conditional kNN $\NNlaPowerCondKnn$. The KL is heavy-tailed (mean
$\NNlaKlMean$~nats, median $\NNlaKlMedian$): many mocks sit far above the null while the rest are
indistinguishable from it, and Sec.~\ref{sec:threshold} shows the latter are those whose amplitude
lies inside the training support. The detectors flag the same mocks: the Spearman correlation
between per-mock KL and combined OOD $p$ is $\NNlaSpearmanKlMeanp$ on NLA, although on the null the
two are uncorrelated ($\rho=\SRhoKlKnnNullProbit$ on the probit scale). Combining them (equal-weight
Stouffer) does not help, AUROC $\SCombNlaKlPlusKnnOne$ against $\SCombNlaKnnOne$ for kNN alone: both
respond to the same physical departure and the KL adds noise, not information.

Conditional kNN ranks last on the IA suites for a definitional reason: the NLA amplitude is a
different parameter from NLA-M's, cannot be conditioned on, and its spread is absorbed into the
conditional null. It is, however, the only score with a hint of signal on $\bg=1.0$
(AUROC $\NGbOnePZeroAurocCondKnn$~[$\NGbOnePZeroAurocCondKnnLo$, $\NGbOnePZeroAurocCondKnnHi$];
mean-$p$ $\NGbOnePZeroAurocMeanp$~[$\NGbOnePZeroAurocMeanpLo$, $\NGbOnePZeroAurocMeanpHi$]), with the
lower bound just above 0.5. Every other interval on $\bg=1.0$, $\bg=1.3$ and variable depth contains
0.5, and the detection powers equal the false-alarm rate: clean null results (Sec.~\ref{sec:bg}).

\paragraph{Cost.} The KL needs at least two encoders and improves slowly with more
(Fig.~\ref{fig:h2h}b: $\NCostNlaKlKTwo$ at $K=2$, $\NCostNlaKlKThree$ at $K=3$, $\NCostNlaKlKFive$ at
$K=5$). One encoder's kNN score already beats five encoders' KL, and pooling five encoders' kNN
$p$-values adds only $+0.007$ ($\NCostNlaKnnKFive$), the $1.14\times$ ceiling above. The reference
cloud can be tiny (Fig.~\ref{fig:refsize}): refitting the whitening and tree on random subsets of the
training summaries, the NLA AUROC is $\SRefcosNlaNFive$ with 5 training cosmologies
($\SRefcosNlaNFiveRows$ summaries), $\SRefcosNlaNTwoZero$ with 20, and saturates at
$\SRefcosNlaNFourZero$ by 40; 100 random summaries spanning all cosmologies give
$\SRefrowsNlaNOneZeroZero$ against $\SRefrowsNlaNOneNineOneSixSeven$ for the full cloud, and the verdict
on the hard suites is unchanged at every size. One encoder plus a few hundred reference summaries is
therefore both the cheaper and the stronger detector for this class of misspecification.





\subsection{How much misspecification is detectable}\label{sec:threshold}
The IA suites span $\Aia\in[-6,6]$ continuously, which makes the amplitude a severity axis.
Figure~\ref{fig:det}a shows that the KL is not a function of ``NLA versus NLA-M'' but of \emph{how far
the amplitude lies from the training support}: its running median is lowest at $\Aia=\NNlaKlArgminA$
(NLA) and $\NNlaZKlArgminA$ (NLA-$z$), next to the training median $\NAiaBandMedian$, where it is
$\NNlaKlMinMedian$~nats, below the in-distribution median. Inside the training band the detection
power is $\NNlaKlPowerInband$~[$\NNlaKlPowerInbandLo$, $\NNlaKlPowerInbandHi$] for the KL and
$\NNlaKnnPowerInband$~[$\NNlaKnnPowerInbandLo$, $\NNlaKnnPowerInbandHi$] for kNN ($\NNlaNInband$ NLA
mocks), consistent with the 5\% false-alarm rate. The change of \emph{model form}, from a mass- and
red-fraction-dependent alignment to a uniform one, is therefore not detectable at matched effective
amplitude; only the amplitude offset is.

Figure~\ref{fig:det}b turns this into a threshold. Walking outward from the training median, half of
all NLA mocks are detected at $\Aia=\NNlaThrKnnFiveZeroDn$~[$\NNlaThrKnnFiveZeroDnLo$,
$\NNlaThrKnnFiveZeroDnHi$] below and $\NNlaThrKnnFiveZeroUp$~[$\NNlaThrKnnFiveZeroUpLo$,
$\NNlaThrKnnFiveZeroUpHi$] above for kNN, and at $\NNlaThrKlFiveZeroDn$~[$\NNlaThrKlFiveZeroDnLo$,
$\NNlaThrKlFiveZeroDnHi$] and $\NNlaThrKlFiveZeroUp$~[$\NNlaThrKlFiveZeroUpLo$, $\NNlaThrKlFiveZeroUpHi$]
for the KL: $\dAia=\NNlaDthrKnnFiveZeroDn$ (kNN) and $\NNlaDthrKlFiveZeroDn$ (KL) towards zero,
$\NNlaDthrKnnFiveZeroUp$ and $\NNlaDthrKlFiveZeroUp$ towards stronger alignment. NLA-$z$ gives the
same picture (50\% power at $\NNlaZThrKnnFiveZeroDn$ and $\NNlaZThrKnnFiveZeroUp$ for kNN,
$\NNlaZThrKlFiveZeroDn$ and $\NNlaZThrKlFiveZeroUp$ for KL). Reliable detection, 80\% power, needs
$\Aia\le\NNlaThrKnnEightZeroDn$ or $\ge\NNlaThrKnnEightZeroUp$ (kNN, NLA), i.e.\
$\dAia\gtrsim\NNlaDthrKnnEightZeroDn$ downward and $\NNlaDthrKnnEightZeroUp$ upward; the KL needs
$\NNlaDthrKlEightZeroDn$ and $\NNlaDthrKlEightZeroUp$. The asymmetry arises because the training
amplitude is strictly positive (minimum $\NAiaBandMin$): an alignment of the wrong sign has no
counterpart in the training suite, whereas a stronger positive one differs only in degree. Mean-$p$ is
markedly weaker on the high side (never 50\% power for NLA within $\Aia\le6$), the conditional score's
loss of IA conditioning noted above. In short, \emph{one unit of $\Aia$ is the resolution of these
detectors at 50\% power; about $1.2$ units towards zero and $2$ (kNN) to $3$ (KL) upward give reliable
detection}. Half a unit is mostly missed: at $\dAia=0.5$ the kNN power is $\NNlaPowerhalfKnnDn$
[$\NNlaPowerhalfKnnDnLo$, $\NNlaPowerhalfKnnDnHi$] towards zero and $\NNlaPowerhalfKnnUp$
[$\NNlaPowerhalfKnnUpLo$, $\NNlaPowerhalfKnnUpHi$] upward (KL: $\NNlaPowerhalfKlDn$ and $\NNlaPowerhalfKlUp$).



\subsection{What is not detectable, and why it matters}\label{sec:bg}
On the scale of Fig.~\ref{fig:det}b, the variable-depth and $\bg$ suites sit at the false-alarm floor:
their powers ($\NVdPowerKl$, $\NGbOnePZeroPowerKl$, $\NGbOnePThreePowerKl$ for KL; $\NVdPowerKnn$,
$\NGbOnePZeroPowerKnn$, $\NGbOnePThreePowerKnn$ for kNN) are indistinguishable from the in-band IA
mocks, i.e.\ below the smallest IA offset the curve resolves, and their AUROC intervals include 0.5
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). The truth-requiring TARP error agrees
once compared with a floor drawn at each suite's own size: at 40 cosmologies $\times$ 2 mocks the
in-distribution floor is $\mathcal{C}=\NFloorFourZeroMean\pm\NFloorFourZeroSd$ (95th percentile
$\NFloorFourZeroPNineFive$), against which $\bg=1.0$ ($\NGbOnePZeroCalMean$) is
$+\NGbOnePZeroCalZfloor$~sd and $\bg=1.3$ ($\NGbOnePThreeCalMean$) $\NGbOnePThreeCalZfloor$~sd;
variable depth is $+\NVdCalZfloor$~sd from its own floor. None is a detection, while the IA suites are
hundreds of sd above theirs ($\mathcal{C}=\NNlaCalMean$, $\NNlaZCalMean$).

For $\bg$ this is largely the correct answer. Against the training prior, $\bg=1.3$ lies at
$z=\NGbOnePThreeBgzBinOne$, $\NGbOnePThreeBgzBinTwo$, $\NGbOnePThreeBgzBinThree$, $\NGbOnePThreeBgzBinFour$,
$\NGbOnePThreeBgzBinFive$, $\NGbOnePThreeBgzBinSix$ in bins 1--6: inside the support of every bin, with a
joint $\chi^2=\BGbOnePThreePriorChiTwo$ for 6 degrees of freedom ($p=\BGbOnePThreePriorChiTwoP$), an
unremarkable draw from the prior. $\bg=1.0$ is out of support in the two highest-redshift bins
($z=\NGbOnePZeroBgzBinFive$, $\NGbOnePZeroBgzBinSix$; $\chi^2=\BGbOnePZeroPriorChiTwo$,
$p=\BGbOnePZeroPriorChiTwoP$), a genuinely out-of-distribution configuration, and it is accordingly
the one hard suite with a marginal conditional signal. That even this configuration is invisible to
the unconditional kNN and to the KL says that a coherent, mild shift of a nuisance field leaves the
8-dimensional summary inside the training cloud.

What matters is what these slices do to the posterior. Because the two $\bg$ suites and the
in-distribution suite share $\BNPairedMocks$ identical mocks (same cosmologies, rotations and noise
realisations), the posterior bias can be compared per mock. In units of the posterior width, the mean
of $(\theta_{\rm true}-\mu_{\rm post})/\sigma_{\rm post}$ over these mocks is
$\BBgpNlaMMeanzOmegaM$~[$\BBgpNlaMMeanzOmegaMLo$, $\BBgpNlaMMeanzOmegaMHi$] in $\Om$ and
$\BBgpNlaMMeanzSigmaEight$ in $\sig$ for the training physics, but $\BGbOnePZeroMeanzOmegaM$~[$\BGbOnePZeroMeanzOmegaMLo$,
$\BGbOnePZeroMeanzOmegaMHi$] and $\BGbOnePZeroMeanzSigmaEight$ for $\bg=1.0$, and
$\BGbOnePThreeMeanzOmegaM$~[$\BGbOnePThreeMeanzOmegaMLo$, $\BGbOnePThreeMeanzOmegaMHi$] and
$\BGbOnePThreeMeanzSigmaEight$ for $\bg=1.3$ (means over the five encoders, which agree to $\le0.1$).
The bias is antisymmetric in $\bg$ and equal in size for the in-support and the out-of-support slice:
the posterior mean tracks the true galaxy bias linearly, by
$\Delta\Om=\NDthetaOmegaM\pm\NDthetaOmegaMSd$, $\Delta\sig=+\NDthetaSigmaEight\pm\NDthetaSigmaEightSd$ and
$\Delta w_0=+\NDthetaWZero\pm\NDthetaWZeroSd$ between the two suites ($\NDthetaOmegaMRatio$,
$+\NDthetaSigmaEightRatio$ and $+\NDthetaWZeroRatio$ posterior widths; in-distribution
$\sigma_{\rm post}=\NSigpostOmegaM$, $\NSigpostSigmaEight$, $\NSigpostWZero$). Two facts constrain the
reading. The TARP calibration of the in-support slice is at the in-distribution floor, so the implicit
marginalisation over $\bg$ has not failed in the sense of mis-stated posterior widths. And a
$\bg$-marginalised posterior is calibrated only on average over the $\bg$ prior: at any \emph{fixed}
$\bg$ its mean is conditionally biased by however much the summaries are degenerate with $\bg$, which
here is a shift of $0.8\,\sigma_{\rm post}$ in $\Om$ and $\sig$ at $\bg$ values that the prior itself
considers ordinary. The out-of-support slice adds no measurable extra bias beyond this linear trend. The trend does not,
however, fully account for the training physics: the line through the two slices predicts
$\BLinpredMeanzOmegaM$ in $\Om$ and $\BLinpredMeanzSigmaEight$ in $\sig$ at the prior-mean bias
$\bg=\BBgPriorMeanAvg$, against $\BBgpNlaMMeanzOmegaM$~[$\BBgpNlaMMeanzOmegaMLo$, $\BBgpNlaMMeanzOmegaMHi$] and
$\BBgpNlaMMeanzSigmaEight$~[$\BBgpNlaMMeanzSigmaEightLo$, $\BBgpNlaMMeanzSigmaEightHi$] observed. Part of the
slices' bias is therefore specific to what both share and the prior rarely produces: one value of $\bg$
in all six bins, a coherent tilt against the prior's rise of $\bg$ with redshift. In that sense the
slices are mildly out-of-distribution in a way no summary-space score registers. For
comparison, the DES~Y3 map-level analysis \cite{jeffrey2025} bounds its source-clustering sensitivity at
$|\Delta\Om|<\NDesBgBoundOmegaM$ with a narrower posterior (0.024); ours is $\NDthetaOmegaMOverDes\times$
larger with a wider one. No detector can catch this, because nothing about such an observation is
unusual: it is the price of a summary that carries source-clustering information without a parameter
to absorb it. Inferring $\bg^{(1..6)}$ explicitly would turn the conditional bias into a reported
$\bg$--$\Om$ degeneracy and let the conditional OOD score see a pinned bias as a parameter shift.

\section{Conclusions}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item \textbf{OOD beats KL, at a fifth of the cost.} One encoder's kNN distance in its own summary
space separates IA-misspecified mocks with AUROC $\NNlaAurocKnn$ and 80\% power at 5\% false alarms;
the five-encoder posterior-disagreement KL reaches $\NNlaAurocKl$ and $\NNlaPowerKl$. Both flag the
same mocks and neither adds to the other; the kNN reference needs only a few hundred training
summaries. The KL remains useful when no summary cloud is retained, but needs $\ge2$ encoders and is the
weaker statistic.
\item \textbf{The detection threshold is about one unit of $\Aia$}: 50\% detection at
$\dAia\approx0.8$--$1.0$ from the training median, 80\% at $\approx1.1$--$1.3$ towards zero and
$\approx2$ (kNN) to $3$ (KL) upward; a change of IA model form at matched amplitude is undetectable.
\item \textbf{Variable depth and the galaxy-bias slices are not detectable}, and for $\bg=1.3$ it
should not be: it is inside the training prior; $\bg=1.0$ is clearly outside in the joint prior
($p=\BGbOnePZeroPriorChiTwoP$), though only through bins 5--6, and still leaves no trace. The
actionable result is that the posterior mean tracks the true $\bg$ ($\Delta\Om=\NDthetaOmegaM$ over the
two slices, $\NDthetaOmegaMOverDes\times$ the DES~Y3 bound, $\pm0.8\,\sigma_{\rm post}$ at fixed $\bg$)
while its calibration stays at the floor: partly the conditional bias of a $\bg$-marginalised
posterior at fixed $\bg$, partly the response to a coherent across-bin $\bg$ configuration the prior
rarely produces. Neither is flagged by any OOD statistic, and both must be addressed on the inference
side (an explicit or wider $\bg$ model), not with a better detector.
\item \textbf{Limitations.} Results hold for this encoder family at 300 training cosmologies; the
dependence on \emph{training}-set size (as opposed to reference-cloud size) was not measured. The $\bg$
suites have 40 cosmologies, so AUROC differences below $\sim$0.07 are unresolved there. On real data the
kNN score is evaluated as is; the conditional score needs posterior draws of $\theta$.
\end{enumerate}
